1 · Problem and users

What problem are you actually solving?

Learners don’t know their real level, so they follow generic roadmaps and waste weeks on things they already know or skip what they don’t. Skilly measures first with a short timed test, then builds a week-by-week plan from those results.

Who is your target user?

Indian engineering students preparing for placements first, then working professionals switching roles, and complete beginners choosing a first language. Those are the three journeys you see on the home page.

How do you know this problem is real?

It’s our own experience and our classmates’: a first-year and a topper get the same 40-item roadmap. Fill in If you’ve done a survey or talked to students, quote the real number here. If not, say “our next step is to validate with a pilot batch”.

Why would a student use this instead of just asking ChatGPT for a roadmap?

ChatGPT gives a plan based on what you say about yourself. Skilly gives a plan based on what you actually answered correctly, with the evidence shown topic by topic. Every priority is a sum you can check, gap × weight, not a guess.

2 · Product

Walk us through the user journey.

Choose Professional, Student or Beginner, then pick languages, take a timed test (8, 12 or 16 questions in 10, 15 or 20 minutes), read an honest report, get a weekly roadmap, then practise with tasks, quizzes and courses. Beginners skip the test and land on a four-week starter path.

What’s different between the three journeys?

Professionals can choose Easy, Medium or Hard and get a detailed weak-topic roadmap. Students get Easy or Medium plus weekly tasks and quizzes. Beginners never face a test: they pick a language, or a goal and we recommend one.

Why don’t students get Hard questions?

Hard questions test production-level knowledge that students usually haven’t needed yet. Failing them gives a discouraging result that isn’t useful. Students can still switch to Professional if they want the challenge.

How many languages and questions do you have?

14 languages, including Python, Java, C++, JavaScript, SQL, Go, Rust and Kotlin. 672 original questions, 16 per language per level. Each has one correct answer and an explanation.

What are the 23 career paths?

9 in software and web, 4 in data and AI, and 10 in core engineering: maths, embedded and IoT, VLSI, electrical, mechanical, civil, chemical, robotics, aerospace and biotech. Each step has an overview, skills, free courses (NPTEL, MIT OCW, official docs) and a mini-project.

Why include core engineering branches? Most platforms are just coding.

Most students in Indian engineering colleges aren’t in CS, and they get ignored by these platforms. We give them a structured path built on NPTEL and MIT OCW, which they already trust.

Are the 3D bubbles just decoration?

They make the site feel welcoming, which matters for beginners. But they turn off during a test, respect reduced-motion settings, and fall back to CSS without WebGL. Nothing important depends on them.

3 · Assessment accuracy

Can 12 questions really tell someone’s level?

Not a global level, and we never claim one. The verdict is scoped: “strong at Medium on 12 Python questions”. Topics with only one question are marked as needing more evidence. That honesty is the point.

Who wrote the questions? Are they validated?

We wrote them. A script checks every one for a unique ID, four distinct options and one correct answer. Expert review by faculty is the first item on our four-week roadmap. We haven’t done that yet.

What stops someone cheating, for example searching the answers?

Skilly is for self-improvement, not certification, so cheating only fools yourself. Still, option order is shuffled per attempt, the clock is a stored deadline that survives a refresh, and submit can’t be counted twice. For college use we’d add proctoring later.

What happens if I refresh the page or my laptop dies mid-test?

The test resumes with the same questions, the same option order and the real remaining time, because the deadline is stored as a timestamp. If time ran out, it auto-submits. Demo it

Why rule-based scoring and not AI grading?

Rules are explainable and cost nothing per user. A student can see exactly why SQL ranks first. AI is better used to read job postings and extract skills, which the original SkillBridge prototype does with keyword matching and TF-IDF.

Is the test adaptive?

Not yet. You pick a level and get a fixed set. Adaptive difficulty (one level up after a right answer, one down after a wrong one) was in the SkillBridge design and is on our six-month roadmap.

4 · The SkillBridge engine

Explain how the roadmap priority is calculated.

For each skill, priority = gap × weight. The gap is how many levels you are below the role’s target. The weight is the share of that role’s job posts that mention the skill. Readiness is the weighted share of target levels you already meet.

Give a concrete example.

Our demo Data Analyst learner is 43% ready. SQL is 3 levels below target with weight 0.9, so priority 2.7, and it ranks first. The total plan is 61 hours: about 11 weeks at 6 hours a week, or 6 weeks at 12. Demo it

Where do the weights come from? Are they real?

Today they come from sample job postings: SQL appears in 9 of 10 Data Analyst samples, so its weight is 0.9. The samples are synthetic, and we say so. Replacing them with real public postings is our first next step.

How do prerequisites work?

If a skill has a prerequisite you also have a gap in, the prerequisite is scheduled first, for example HTML and CSS before JavaScript. The hours are then packed into weeks according to your weekly time.

How does Round 3 relate to your Round 1 SkillBridge submission?

SkillBridge was the gap-analysis engine. Skilly ports it exactly (our tests reproduce the same numbers) and wraps it in a complete product: the assessment, report, weekly roadmap, courses and the beginner journey.

5 · Technical

What’s your tech stack?

React 18 with Vite, and Three.js for the bubbles. It’s a static site with hash routing, so it runs on any free host and works offline once loaded. The course catalogue and 3D scene load only when needed.

Where is user data stored? Why no backend?

In the browser’s local storage, validated and versioned on every load. No server means no login, no personal data collected, no running cost, and nothing to go down during a demo. Accounts and sync come with the college pilot.

Doesn’t local storage mean users lose progress if they switch devices?

Yes. That’s a deliberate trade-off for a privacy-first prototype. The fix is optional accounts with cloud sync, which we need anyway for the placement-cell dashboard.

How did you test it?

18 automated logic checks covering scoring, roadmaps and the SkillBridge numbers. 672 questions validated, and 161 course links checked to load. We also walked every journey by hand: refresh mid-test, timer expiry, double submit, empty survey, phone width, no WebGL.

What was the hardest bug?

Switching from a long career path to a shorter one crashed the viewer, because it kept the old step index. We fixed it by resetting the view per path. Another one: Beginner skipped the language question entirely, and we caught it in testing.

Where does the course content come from?

1,630 topics with 3,899 resources from roadmap.sh’s open roadmaps, cached for the weekly plans, plus curated free courses (NPTEL, MIT OCW, official docs). We link to and credit the original sources rather than copying them.

Is it mobile-friendly and accessible?

Yes. It’s tested at 375 px phone width, works with the keyboard, has a reduced-motion mode, and has a CSS fallback when 3D isn’t available. Demo it on a phone if asked.

6 · Business and scale

How will you make money?

It stays free for learners. Colleges pay for a placement-cell dashboard that shows where their whole batch stands before placement season, plus downloadable batch reports.

How does it scale to a lakh users?

Static files on a CDN, so there’s almost no cost per extra user and no server to overload. When accounts are added, only the sync layer needs a backend.

Who are your competitors?

Static roadmap sites like roadmap.sh, quiz platforms, and course platforms. Roadmap sites give everyone the same list, and quiz sites give a score but no next step. Skilly connects the two: test, then a plan from the results. We build on open resources rather than replacing them.

What stops a big platform copying this?

Nothing stops them copying features. Our advantage is focus: Indian engineering students across all branches, NPTEL-based paths, regional languages next, and college partnerships for the dashboard data.

How would you get your first 1,000 users?

Through our own college and nearby ones: placement cells, coding clubs and department WhatsApp groups. A free batch report for a placement cell gets a whole class onboarded at once.

7 · Future and impact

What would you build next with more time?

In the next 4 weeks: real job-post data and expert question review. In 3 months: accounts and the placement-cell dashboard. In 6 months: Hindi and regional languages, adaptive tests and mentor review of projects.

How will you measure if Skilly works?

Re-test scores after a few weeks on the plan, the share of weekly tasks completed, and, in the pilot, placement-round results compared with students not using it.

What’s the social impact?

Students from tier-2 and tier-3 colleges without mentors get a free, honest picture of where they stand and what to do this week, in any engineering branch, with no login or payment.

8 · Tough or tricky questions

This looks like a nice frontend. Where’s the innovation?

The innovation is the link between evidence and plan. A scoped, honest verdict feeds an explainable priority formula, gap × weight, that turns a 12-question test into a schedule in hours and weeks. Open the report and the planner to show it.

Your data is synthetic. Isn’t the whole plan meaningless then?

The engine is real and tested. Only the input weights are samples. Swap in real postings and the same formula gives real priorities. We chose to be transparent about that rather than present made-up data as real.

What’s the biggest weakness of your project right now?

Have a real answer ready, for example: “The questions haven’t had expert review, and the job weights are samples. Both are the first items on our roadmap.” Judges trust teams that know their limits.

What if your live demo breaks?

It runs with no server, so a local copy on the laptop works without Wi-Fi. Keep one ready, and keep the PDF deck as a backup.
