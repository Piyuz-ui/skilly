1 · Hook and problem

0:00 – 0:30
Slides: Cover → Problem

Good [morning/afternoon]. We’re [Team name], and this is Skilly. Quick question: how many of you, as students, followed a roadmap with forty items on it and never knew which ones you could skip? (pause) That’s the problem. A topper and a first-year get the same list. Mock tests give you a score but no next step. And beginners freeze before writing a single line of code, because they don’t even know which language to start with.

2 · The idea

0:30 – 1:00
Slide: Insight

Skilly does one thing differently: measure first, then plan from the evidence. A short, timed test becomes an honest report, and that report becomes a week-by-week roadmap with real, free courses. Every recommendation tells you why it’s there. It’s built on our Round 1 SkillBridge gap engine, now a complete product. Let me show you.

Switch to the browser. The live site should already be open on the home page.

3 · Live demo

1:00 – 3:30
Live website · one person clicks, one person talks

1:00 Home: pop one bubble, scroll once so the logo squashes.

Skilly opens with one question: which best describes you? Professional, Student or Beginner. Three different journeys.

1:15 Click Beginner → choose the goal “Placement interviews (DSA)” → it recommends C++ → open week 1.

A beginner never faces a test. They pick a language from fourteen, or just a goal, and get a four-week starter path. Week one even has the install links.

1:40 Switch to Student → pick SQL → show that Hard isn’t offered → 8 questions, 10 minutes → Start. Answer three quickly, leave the rest blank → Submit.

Students get Easy or Medium. There are 672 original questions across 14 languages. The clock is a stored deadline, so refreshing the page doesn’t buy extra time. Watch: I’ll submit with blanks.

2:20 Report: point at marks, the accuracy line and the topic breakdown → open one explanation.

An honest report: correct, wrong and unanswered shown separately, broken down by topic, and every answer has an explanation. The verdict only claims what the test showed, never a global label.

2:40 Click through to the roadmap → show the week bubbles → tick one task.

The weakest topics come first, as weekly tasks, a quiz and matched courses from roadmap.sh.

2:55 Career paths → filter Core engineering (VLSI, Civil, Mechanical are visible) → open Data Analyst → “Load the demo learner”.

We cover 23 career paths, including ten core engineering branches most platforms ignore. Here’s the SkillBridge engine: this learner is 43% ready. SQL ranks first because they’re three levels short and nine in ten job posts ask for it: gap times weight, 2.7. That’s 61 hours, about 11 weeks at 6 hours a week. Every number here is a sum you can check.

3:30 Switch back to the slides.

4 · Built and tested

3:30 – 4:15
Slides: How it’s built → Testing

Under the hood: React and Three.js, with a custom shader for the bubbles. There’s no server: everything runs in your browser. So there’s no login, no personal data leaves your device, and it costs nothing to run. It works on phones, tablets and laptops. And we tested it like a product: 18 automated logic checks, all 672 questions validated, 161 course links checked, and every journey walked end to end, including refreshing mid-test and letting the timer run out.

5 · What’s next

4:15 – 4:45
Slide: What’s next

To be honest, today’s job-market weights come from sample postings. So next: real job postings, faculty review of the questions, and a pilot with [one college / number students]. After that comes a placement-cell dashboard showing where a whole batch stands before placement season. That’s also how Skilly sustains itself: free for students, paid by colleges. And then Hindi and regional languages.

6 · Close

4:45 – 5:00
Slide: Closing

Skilly: know where you stand, and know what to practise this week. It’s live right now at [your link]. Thank you!

Stop talking. Smile. Wait for questions.

If you’re running over or under time

  • Running long? In the demo, skip the Beginner step (saves 25 s) and only ever drop the testing sentences, never the close.
  • Running short? On the report, open a second explanation, or on career paths drag the hours slider to 12 h/week and show the plan shrink from 11 to 6 weeks.
  • Timekeeper: one member silently shows fingers at 3:30 (demo should be ending) and 4:30 (start closing).

Before you go on stage

  • Open the live site in a fresh browser profile or private window so old tests don’t appear. Close other tabs and turn on Do Not Disturb.
  • Zoom the browser to about 110% so the back row can read it. Keep the slides in a second window and practise switching with Cmd+Tab.
  • If the Wi-Fi fails: run the site from the laptop (npm run dev), or present the demo from the slides: Assessment, Report and Planner already show the real numbers.
  • Rehearse the whole thing at least three times with a timer. The demo clicks should be muscle memory.
  • Keep the Q&A PDF handy for the questions afterwards.
